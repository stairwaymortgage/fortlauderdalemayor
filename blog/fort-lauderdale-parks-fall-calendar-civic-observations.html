October in the Parks: What Fort Lauderdale's Fall Calendar Tells Me About This City

A look at Fort Lauderdale's packed October–November parks calendar and what it signals about community investment and public space.

By Jim Blackburn · October 14, 2026

Every month I make a point of reading the City's parks programming bulletin cover to cover. Not because I'm planning my weekend — though sometimes I am — but because public parks programming is one of the clearest, least-filtered signals of what a city actually values. This month's edition gave me a lot to think about.

A Lot Happening Across a Lot of Neighborhoods

According to the City's October 2026 Playbook bulletin — the Parks & Recreation Department's official digital program guide — Fort Lauderdale has lined up a dense stretch of community events between now and early November. The list, available at parks.fortlauderdale.gov, spans multiple parks and neighborhoods: a Big Toy and Truck Extravaganza at Snyder Park on October 10th (10 AM–2 PM), an evening concert featuring the Larry Dogg Band at Carter Park on October 16th (7–10 PM), simultaneous Fall Festivals at both Croissant Park and Riverland Park on October 24th, and the Fort LauderBoo Halloween event also on the 24th at Snyder Park. A free Diwali Festival of Lights is scheduled for Las Olas Oceanside Park on October 25th (6–9 PM), followed by a Water Safety Day at the Fort Lauderdale Aquatic Center on November 3rd, and the Light Up Lauderdale holiday kickoff at Esplanade Park on November 5th.

What I notice immediately is the geographic spread. Snyder Park, Carter Park, Croissant Park, Riverland Park, Las Olas Oceanside Park, Esplanade Park — these aren't all the same neighborhood. That distribution matters. A parks calendar that rotates events across multiple corners of the city is doing something different from one that concentrates programming in a single showcase venue. Whether that distribution is intentional equity or just scheduling logistics, I don't know. But it's worth tracking over time.

The Club 55+ Detail That Stood Out

The bulletin also recaps the Club 55+ program, which the City launched to give adults 55 and older access to programming at 12 city parks. The membership rate, per the City's announcement, is $55 annually for both residents and non-residents, with fee reductions available for qualifying residents. The activity menu is genuinely broad — tai chi, creative writing, ballroom dance, pickleball, Spanish language classes, golf, and more — with scheduled outings running through February 2027, including visits to the NSU Art Museum, the Street Painting Festival, and Tree Tops Park.

I keep returning to this one. $55 a year for access to programming across a dozen parks is, by any measure, a low-barrier entry point. For older residents living on fixed incomes in a city where the cost of living has climbed steadily, that kind of structured, affordable social programming isn't a luxury — it's infrastructure. Isolation among older adults is a documented public health concern, and parks departments are often the most consistent point of civic contact many seniors have. That's not a small thing.

What I'm Watching for Going Forward

A few questions I'm carrying into future dispatches as I continue this 14-year civic project:

  • Attendance and access data: The programming looks strong on paper. Are residents in every part of the city actually showing up? Transportation to parks is a real barrier for some households.
  • Aquatic Center capacity: The bulletin notes that the Jimmy Evert Tennis Center has limited court availability due to annual restoration work. Routine maintenance is good. But I want to understand the longer-term capital condition of our recreational facilities — especially the Aquatic Center on Seabreeze Blvd., which serves a broad population.
  • After-school programming reach: The bulletin flags a licensed, accredited after-school program for kids in grades K–5, running Monday through Friday from 2–6 PM. That's a significant public service. I'd like to know capacity, waitlists, and whether demand is being met across neighborhoods.

None of this is a criticism. A city that publishes a transparent monthly program guide and populates its parks with free and low-cost events is doing something right. My job — as a resident watching this city for the long haul — is to keep asking whether the investment is reaching everyone it should.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find the full Fort Lauderdale Parks & Recreation October 2026 program guide?
The City of Fort Lauderdale publishes its monthly Playbook program guide through the Parks & Recreation Department. The October 2026 edition, including event details and registration links, is available at parks.fortlauderdale.gov.

No. This is a completely independent personal civic project. I am Jim Blackburn, a private Fort Lauderdale resident documenting my observations of city governance as part of a long-term pre-candidacy study project. I have no affiliation with the City, its departments, or any elected official.
Who is eligible for the Club 55+ fee reduction, and how does someone sign up?
According to the City's October Playbook bulletin, Club 55+ membership is $55 per year for residents and non-residents, with fee reductions available for qualifying residents. Registration is described as ongoing. For eligibility details and enrollment, the City directs residents to parks.fortlauderdale.gov.
