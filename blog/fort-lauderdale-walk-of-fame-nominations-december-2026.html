Neighborhood Preservation

Nominate a Fort Lauderdale Legend Before December 14

The City's Walk of Fame is accepting 2027 honoree nominations through Dec. 14, 2026. Here's why this program matters to neighborhood identity.

By Jim Blackburn · October 19, 2026

Illustration

Every now and then a civic program comes along that does something deceptively simple: it asks a community to stop and say thank you. The Fort Lauderdale Walk of Fame is one of those programs, and its 2027 nomination window closes at 11:59 p.m. on Monday, December 14, 2026. I've been watching this city long enough — and tracking it closely enough through this multi-year civic project — to believe that who a community chooses to honor tells you a great deal about who that community thinks it is.

What the Program Actually Is

According to the City bulletin published at fortlauderdale.gov, the Walk of Fame was established in 2010, launched at the inaugural Great American Beach Party as a tribute to actress and singer Connie Francis on the 50th anniversary of the 1960 film Where the Boys Are — the movie that turned Fort Lauderdale into a nationally recognized destination almost overnight. Each honoree receives a commemorative paver installed along the east side of A1A at Las Olas Boulevard, creating a permanent, publicly accessible memorial to their contributions. New honorees are traditionally announced at the Great American Beach Party, tentatively set for the Saturday of Memorial Day Weekend 2027.

Who Qualifies — and Why the Criteria Matter

The City outlines several broad categories of impact that make someone nomination-worthy. These include making a sustained positive difference in the lives of young people, contributing to quality of life for residents, fostering community spirit, bringing positive recognition to Fort Lauderdale at a local or national level, and encouraging visitors to choose Fort Lauderdale as a destination. That list is deliberately wide. It's not just for entertainers or elected officials. A longtime youth coach, a neighborhood organizer, a small-business owner who anchored a corridor through hard times — any of these people could qualify.

That breadth is worth pausing on. The criteria overlap significantly with the five issues I track on this site — particularly neighborhood preservation and community resilience. The people who hold neighborhoods together, who mentor the next generation, who show up at civic meetings when nobody's watching — they rarely make headlines. A program like this is one of the few formal mechanisms a city has to say their work counts.

My Civic Take

I'm not here to tell you who to nominate. That's the community's call, and it should be. What I will say is this: December 14 is a firm deadline, and these programs often receive far fewer nominations than they deserve simply because residents assume someone else will handle it. If you've watched a neighbor, a teacher, a coach, or a community builder pour years into making Fort Lauderdale better, this is a concrete way to make sure that work doesn't go unrecognized.

From a longer-term civic lens — the one I'm applying through this 14-year project — programs like the Walk of Fame are part of the soft infrastructure of a city. Flood walls and affordable housing units are essential, full stop. But so is the intentional act of honoring people who carry the city's character forward. Both matter. Neither replaces the other.

If you have questions about the nomination process, the City bulletin lists Todd Stilphen as the contact at 954-828-5408 or tstilphen@fortlauderdale.gov. The nomination form is available through the City's official website.

Bottom Line

  • Deadline: Monday, December 14, 2026 at 11:59 p.m.
  • What happens next: Honorees announced at the Great American Beach Party, tentatively Memorial Day Weekend 2027
  • Where the pavers live: Along A1A on the east side at Las Olas Boulevard
  • How to nominate: Online through the City of Fort Lauderdale's official website

Think about who in your neighborhood deserves one of those pavers. Then submit the nomination before the clock runs out.
