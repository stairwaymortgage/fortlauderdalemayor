Flood Infrastructure

King Tides Oct. 24–31: The Year's Highest Expected Tuesday Morning

Fort Lauderdale's king tide cycle peaks Oct. 27 around 9:45 AM. Here's what I'm watching and why it matters for the long game.

By Jim Blackburn · October 23, 2026

Illustration

I keep a tide calendar on my desk the way some people keep a weather app open on their phone. For anyone paying close attention to how Fort Lauderdale's streets hold up under pressure, the last week of October 2026 is a week to watch closely.

What the City Is Warning About

According to the City of Fort Lauderdale's bulletin, king tides are expected to bring elevated water levels across the city from Saturday, October 24 through Saturday, October 31. The City notes that compounding factors — wind direction, ocean currents, and the longer-term trend of sea level rise — could push tidal heights to potential record elevations during this cycle. The City identifies Tuesday, October 27 at approximately 9:45 AM as the single highest anticipated tide of the entire year. That's a significant benchmark, and it lands on a weekday morning when streets are full of commuters. You can find the City's full guidance and reporting tools at fortlauderdale.gov/fixitftl.

The City's published peak tide schedule runs twice daily through most of the week:

  • Saturday, Oct. 24: 7:15 AM and 7:30 PM
  • Sunday, Oct. 25: 8:00 AM and 8:15 PM
  • Monday, Oct. 26: 8:45 AM and 9:00 PM
  • Tuesday, Oct. 27: 9:45 AM and 9:45 PM
  • Wednesday, Oct. 28: 10:30 AM and 10:30 PM
  • Thursday, Oct. 29: 11:15 AM and 11:30 PM
  • Friday, Oct. 30: 12:15 PM
  • Saturday, Oct. 31: 12:30 AM and 1:15 PM

Three Practical Asks from the City — and My Read on Each

The City bulletin translates the forecast into three resident actions, and I think all three are worth unpacking:

  • Drive cautiously and avoid standing water. The City specifically calls out the vehicle wake problem — driving fast through flooded streets pushes water toward nearby homes. This is a real and underappreciated issue in low-lying neighborhoods. It's not just about your own car; it's about the water you're displacing onto your neighbor's doorstep.
  • Protect your property with sandbags and elevation. The City advises residents in wake-prone areas to place sandbags at entry points and move vulnerable belongings to higher ground before peak times, not after. Reactive responses during an active flood event are far less effective.
  • Secure your waste carts. This one sounds small but matters operationally. Carts floating into roadways during a flood event create hazards and complicate cleanup. The City is asking residents to hold off on curbside placement if their street floods, or to retrieve carts immediately after collection.

Reporting Impassable Roads

The City has set up multiple channels for residents to flag streets that become impassable during this king tide window. You can call 954-828-8000, email the City's service team, or submit a report through the FixIt FTL app. That reporting data matters — it's one of the most direct ways residents can contribute to the City's understanding of which streets flood worst and how quickly water recedes. I've written before on past dispatches about how citizen-reported data can shape infrastructure prioritization, and king tide events are exactly when that data gets generated.

The Bigger Pattern I'm Tracking

This is now at least the third king tide bulletin I've documented this fall season, and each one adds to the picture I'm building as part of this long-term civic project. The City noting that this week's tides could reach potential record elevations — not just seasonal highs, but all-time highs — is the detail that sticks with me. That framing isn't alarmist; it reflects real trends in sea level data. For a city with the street grid and drainage age that Fort Lauderdale has, record tides are a stress test of infrastructure that was never designed for this baseline.

Flood infrastructure is one of the five issues I'm tracking across all of these dispatches precisely because it's the issue with the longest lead time. The decisions made today about pump capacity, road elevation, and stormwater design won't fully pay off for a decade or more. That's why I think steady, year-over-year documentation of events like this week's king tide cycle is worth doing — even when it feels repetitive. The repetition is the point.

Frequently asked questions

When is the highest tide expected during the October 24–31 king tide cycle?
According to the City of Fort Lauderdale's bulletin, the peak morning tide on Tuesday, October 27 at approximately 9:45 AM is expected to be the highest tide of the entire year. Residents in flood-prone areas should plan accordingly well before that time.
How can Fort Lauderdale residents report a street that becomes impassable during king tides?
The City offers three reporting options: call 954-828-8000, email the City's customer service team, or submit a request through the FixIt FTL app at fortlauderdale.gov/fixitftl. Reporting helps the City track which streets are most affected.
