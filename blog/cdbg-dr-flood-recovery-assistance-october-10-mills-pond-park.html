Flood Infrastructure

One More Chance: Oct. 10 CDBG-DR Help Session for April 2023 Flood Survivors

Fort Lauderdale is hosting in-person CDBG-DR application help on Oct. 10. Here's why I think every eligible resident should show up.

By Jim Blackburn · October 15, 2026

Illustration

Eighteen months after the April 2023 flood turned streets into rivers and pushed families out of their homes, I still hear from neighbors who quietly assumed the window for help had closed. This week's City bulletin was a reminder that it hasn't — and that assumption may be costing people real money.

What the City Is Offering on October 10

According to the City of Fort Lauderdale's official bulletin (fortlauderdale.gov), the City is hosting a free, in-person application assistance event on Saturday, October 10, from 10 a.m. to 3:30 p.m. at Mills Pond Park, 2201 NW 9th Ave. The event is designed to help residents work through the application process for the City's Community Development Block Grant Disaster Recovery (CDBG-DR) Program — a federally backed program that channels disaster recovery dollars to eligible homeowners. City staff will be on-site to walk people through paperwork and answer questions.

The four types of assistance available under CDBG-DR include home rehabilitation, home reconstruction, home elevation, and reimbursement for eligible repair costs. That's a meaningful range — and elevation assistance in particular is something I've written about in the context of long-term flood infrastructure and resilience, because elevating a structure is one of the most durable investments a homeowner can make against future surge events.

Who Should Actually Go

Here's the part that surprised me enough to flag it as a separate point: the City explicitly states that residents are encouraged to attend even if they have already completed repairs, already received FEMA or insurance payouts, are missing documents, or have outstanding questions about permits. That's a much wider net than most people assume. If you fixed your flooded floor out of pocket and then received a partial FEMA check, you may still be eligible for some form of reimbursement or supplemental assistance.

The guidance is simple: bring whatever documentation you have related to your household income and flood damage. City staff can identify what else might be needed. Starting with an incomplete file is fine — that's exactly what the event is for.

Why This Matters in the Larger Picture

I've been tracking Fort Lauderdale's recovery arc since that storm as part of this long-term civic project, and one pattern I keep observing is an information gap: residents who qualify for programs don't apply because they assume they don't, or because the process feels too complicated to start alone. CDBG-DR funds come with federal strings — income limits, documentation requirements, inspection steps — and that complexity is real. But leaving eligible dollars on the table doesn't just hurt individual households; it slows the neighborhood-scale recovery the whole city needs.

Fort Lauderdale's low-lying geography means flooding is a structural challenge, not a one-time crisis. Programs like CDBG-DR that fund elevation and reconstruction are part of how the city begins to harden its housing stock against the next event. Every home that gets elevated or properly rehabilitated is one fewer household in emergency mode when the next major rain system arrives. That's not an abstraction — it's a measurable reduction in post-storm displacement and cost.

What I'd Encourage You to Do

  • If you were affected by the April 2023 flood and haven't applied, go to Mills Pond Park on October 10. Bring what you have.
  • If you started an application and got stuck, this is your opportunity to get unstuck with City staff in the room.
  • If you completed repairs using your own funds, ask specifically about reimbursement eligibility — don't assume prior spending disqualifies you.
  • If you know a neighbor who was affected, share this information with them directly. Word-of-mouth fills the gaps that official bulletins sometimes miss.

I'll keep watching how this program unfolds and what participation looks like as the application period continues. Can I apply for CDBG-DR assistance if I already received FEMA or insurance money after the April 2023 flood?
According to the City's bulletin, yes — residents who have already received FEMA or insurance assistance are still encouraged to attend and explore whether they qualify for additional help under the CDBG-DR program. City staff at the October 10 event can assess individual situations.
What if I don't have all my documents ready before October 10?
The City's bulletin specifically states that missing documents are not a reason to stay home. Residents should bring whatever they have — household income records and flood damage documentation are a good starting point — and City staff will help identify what else may be needed.
