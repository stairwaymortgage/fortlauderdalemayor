Flood Infrastructure

King Tides Return Oct. 7–13: What I'm Watching as a Fort Lauderdale Resident

King tides are back this week. Here's how I'm tracking the twice-daily flood windows and what they reveal about our city's resilience.

By Jim Blackburn · October 14, 2026

I keep a running note on my phone every time the City pushes out a tide advisory. Not because I enjoy doomscrolling water levels, but because these weekly windows — twice a day, like clockwork — are the single clearest stress test our streets and storm drains get all year. This week, the test runs from Wednesday, October 7 through Tuesday, October 13.

What the City Is Saying

According to the City of Fort Lauderdale's official bulletin, a king tide cycle is underway and elevated water levels are expected throughout the week, with two peak windows each day. The City notes that while projections suggest slightly lower water than we saw in late September, minor street flooding remains a real possibility — especially if rain arrives close to a peak tide window. The City is directing residents to report impassable roads through the FixIt FTL app at fortlauderdale.gov/fixitftl, by phone at 954-828-8000, or by email. Those are the official channels, and they matter.

The peak tide windows listed in the bulletin shift progressively later each morning and evening across the week. On Wednesday the 7th, they fall around 6:15 AM and 6:45 PM. By Tuesday the 13th, they've shifted to roughly 10:45 AM and 10:45 PM. That drift is predictable lunar mechanics — but it has real implications for commuters, school drop-offs, and businesses near low-lying corridors.

Why I Track This as a Long-Term Civic Project

Part of what I'm doing with this blog — a personal, independent documentation effort toward a possible 2040 mayoral run — is building a ground-level record of how Fort Lauderdale handles recurring, predictable stressors. King tides are not a surprise. They happen every fall, often intensifying through October and November. They are the low-stakes rehearsal for what a stronger storm surge or a major rain event would do to the same streets.

What I want to understand over time: Which neighborhoods get the first calls? Which roads repeatedly show up in FixIt FTL reports? Is the City's drainage infrastructure improving in measurable ways from one king tide season to the next? I can't answer those questions yet — I'm an observer, not a policymaker — but I can start asking them systematically and publicly.

What Residents Can Do This Week

  • Know your peak windows. The twice-daily high points shift later each day this week. If you live near a canal, a low-lying street, or any area that has flooded before, plan your travel around those windows rather than through them.
  • Don't drive through standing water. The City's guidance on this is unambiguous, and it's right. Standing water conceals depth, debris, and open drain grates. It's not worth it.
  • Report what you see. The FixIt FTL app at fortlauderdale.gov/fixitftl is the official City tool. Every report you submit creates a data point that — in theory — informs where the City prioritizes drainage work. Use it.
  • Watch the rain forecast. The City bulletin explicitly notes that rainfall coinciding with peak tides will worsen ponding. The two factors compound each other. Keep an eye on the hourly forecast through the week.

The Bigger Picture I'm Building Toward

Fort Lauderdale is a coastal city that has known for decades it would face accelerating tidal pressure. The investments needed in pump stations, road elevation, green infrastructure, and canal management are enormous — and they take time. I'm not here to criticize any individual decision or official. I'm here to document what the city looks and feels like at street level, season after season, so that by 2040 there's an honest record of progress, stagnation, or regression. King tide week is a chapter in that record. I'll be watching.

How do I report a flooded or impassable road in Fort Lauderdale during king tides?
According to the City of Fort Lauderdale's bulletin, residents can report impassable roads using the FixIt FTL app at fortlauderdale.gov/fixitftl, by calling 954-828-8000, or by emailing cservice@fortlauderdale.gov.
Why do king tide peak windows shift later each day across the week?
King tides are driven by the gravitational pull of the moon and sun aligning. The lunar cycle causes high tides to occur roughly 50 minutes later each day, which is why the City's bulletin shows peak windows progressively later from Wednesday through Tuesday.
