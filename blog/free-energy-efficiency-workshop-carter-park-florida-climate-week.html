Environmental Resilience

A Free Workshop That Connects Energy Bills to Flood Risk — I'm Going

Fort Lauderdale is hosting a free energy efficiency workshop at Carter Park on Oct. 10. Here's why I think it matters beyond the utility bill.

By Jim Blackburn · October 14, 2026

Saturday mornings are usually my time to walk the neighborhoods I care most about — checking drainage, watching construction, taking notes. But on October 10, I'll be inside Carter Park's recreation room instead, because the City of Fort Lauderdale is offering something I think too many residents will overlook: a free, 90-minute workshop that ties household energy habits directly to flood risk. That combination is exactly the kind of integrated thinking I want to see more of in this city.

What the City Is Actually Offering

According to the City bulletin published at fortlauderdale.gov, the City of Fort Lauderdale is partnering with Dream in Green and the VoLo Foundation to host a free Residential Energy Efficiency Workshop on Saturday, October 10, from 10:00 to 11:30 a.m. at Carter Park Recreation Room, 1450 W. Sunrise Blvd., Fort Lauderdale, FL 33311. The event is an official community offering tied to Florida Climate Week 2026. Registration is encouraged but the event is free to attend.

The session is described as interactive. City staff plan to share local residential energy affordability data — actual numbers, not generalities — alongside information about municipal water conservation and sustainability programs. Participants will also walk away with low-cost, practical steps to reduce monthly utility usage and, notably, some guidance on protecting residential property against localized flooding.

Why the Flood Connection Matters to Me

Most energy efficiency conversations stop at the utility bill. Insulate your attic, swap your bulbs, adjust your thermostat. Fine. Useful. But what caught my attention in this bulletin is the explicit mention of localized flood risk as part of the curriculum. In a city where stormwater infrastructure is already under stress — and where back-to-back heavy rain events have exposed real vulnerabilities block by block — the idea that a homeowner's daily choices connect to how water moves through a neighborhood is not a stretch. It's actually good science.

Water conservation reduces the volume of water entering the municipal system during peak demand. Energy efficiency at home can affect how much heat a neighborhood radiates, which influences localized weather. These aren't radical ideas — they're the kind of systems-level thinking that rarely makes it into a free Saturday workshop. If the City and its partners deliver on this framing, October 10 could be a genuinely valuable morning for residents who live in Fort Lauderdale's more flood-prone corridors.

Carter Park as a Civic Venue

The choice of Carter Park is worth noting. The park sits in a part of the city that has historically seen slower investment in resilience infrastructure compared to wealthier districts closer to the water. Hosting an environmental literacy event there — rather than at a downtown conference room — signals at least an intention to reach the residents who stand to benefit most from lower utility costs and better flood awareness. I'll be paying attention to who actually shows up and whether the City follows this workshop with any targeted outreach or program enrollment support in the surrounding neighborhoods.

How I'm Approaching This as a Long-Term Observer

I'm not affiliated with the City, Dream in Green, or the VoLo Foundation. I'm a Fort Lauderdale resident who has been tracking how the city makes decisions across five issue areas — flood infrastructure, housing affordability, neighborhood preservation, environmental resilience, and economic development — as part of a long-term civic project I started in 2026. Events like this one are data points. They tell me what the City prioritizes, who it tries to reach, and whether the programming reflects the complexity of the problems we actually face.

I'll write a follow-up after October 10 with observations from the room. In the meantime, if you're a Fort Lauderdale resident who pays a utility bill or worries about water in your yard after a hard rain, this workshop seems worth two hours of your Saturday.

What does energy efficiency have to do with flood risk?
The City's workshop curriculum reportedly includes guidance on protecting residential property against localized flood risks alongside energy and water conservation tips.
